In November 1984, Simple Minds recorded Keith Forsey's Dont You (Forget About Me), after initialy declining to record the song but subsequently reconsidering, after other artists like Billy Idol also turned down the offer.

Don't You (Forget About Me) was recorded for the movie The Breakfast Club, a movie written and directed by John Hughes in 1985, before working on other movies like Uncle Buck, starring John Candy, and the christmas classic Home Alone, featuring Macaulay Culkin !
The movie features five high schoolers, all of them from different walks of life, spending a saturday in detention under the supervision of an authoritarian vice principal.

The single became a huge success and it toped the charts in many countries shortly after it's release. Which is funny, because Simple Minds really didn't like this song and didn't think it would be a success whatsoever !
Jim Kerr, the lead vocalist of Simple Minds, stated in an interview with The Guardian, that the band "Couldn't give a toss about teenage American schoolkids.". Simple Minds however agreed to spend a few days together with Keith Forsey to get to know each other a bit better.
During that time, Keith Forsey and the members of Simple Minds became really good friends, bonding over Forsey's previous works in the german experimental music scene, as Keith Forsey used to be the drummer of the krautrock band Amon Duul II. He also worked together with Giorgio Moroder on multiple occasions, helping out with the percussions in Donna Summer's I Feel Love.

The friendship the band built up with Keith Forsey over these few days made the band reconsider and they'd ultimately agree to record Don't you (Forget About Me), which was still just a hastily recorded demo tape at that point in production.

Nowadays the members of Simple Minds have warmed up to the success of this song. Charlie Burchill, the guitarist of the band, stated in that same Guardian interview from earlier: "At the time, we felt the song didn't suit us, because we had delusions of being ultra-hip. When I listen to it now, it's obviously a brilliant, well-crafted pop song. I'm embarrassed we dissed it so much.".

Funnily enough, in 2001 Billy Idol decided to actually came back to this song and wrote his own cover for his Greatest Hits compilation album !
